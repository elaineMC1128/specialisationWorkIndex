Final Instrument

Project Synopsis

This project is a browser based piano designed for toddlers. With eight keys, playful visual feedback and two modes, Free Play and Learning, it invites young children to explore sound through simple, direct interaction. Built around approachability, playfulness, encouragement and guidance, it aims to grow a child’s motivation to explore and their sense of self-efficacy.

Chosen Design Scenario: Musical Toy for Toddlers

Prompt

Design a way for young children with minimal language skills to explore music.

Context

Browser based musical instruments offer users the ability to dynamically perform music and explore complex audio design. While these interactions allow for a depth of control, they can be intimidating for many adult users, let alone young children whose language skills are still developing.

How the Finished Project Responds

Most browser instruments focus on deep control and technical skill, which can overwhelm adults, let alone toddlers whose language is still developing. So this project doesn’t try to teach music theory. Instead, it lets a young child discover that their own actions can make something that sounds good, right away.

To keep things simple, the whole interaction comes down to one action: pressing a key. Since toddlers can’t read instructions, the project opens with a one time, proactive onboarding. Its operational guidance shows the child what to do and encourages them along the way, while the friendly character leading it makes first contact feel approachable rather than intimidating. After that, it steps aside and lets the child play.

In Free Play mode, every sound comes with dynamic light bars. Each coloured key is linked to its own fixed vertical bar, creating a direct spatial and temporal link between the keyboard and the screen. Pressing a key lights up its bar. Holding it makes the bar rise and brighten, and letting go brings it back to rest. This instant response helps the child connect what they did with what they see and hear. At the same time, the random heights and segment patterns mean the same key never looks quite the same twice. This keeps exploring fresh and invites the child to try again and again.

Learning Mode then adds gentle guidance, showing which key to press next to finish a familiar song, with no scores and no wrong answers. Together, the two modes balance free exploration with a clear goal, so every attempt feels like a success.
